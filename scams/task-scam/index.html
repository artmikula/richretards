The Task Scam: "Get Paid to Like Videos" Is Stealing Billions

Published June 29, 2026 | By Rich Retards

It usually arrives as a text from a number you do not know. "Hi, I am hiring for a remote position. Earn $300 a day doing simple online tasks. Flexible hours. Interested?" Sometimes it is a WhatsApp message, sometimes a job board reply, sometimes a stranger who says they got your number from a recruiter.

The job sounds insultingly easy. Like videos. Rate hotels. Boost product listings. Tap a button, watch a number go up, get paid. And here is the part that makes this scam so effective: at first, you actually do get paid.

This is the task scam, and in 2026 it is one of the fastest growing frauds on the planet. It has drained billions because it does the one thing most scams cannot. It pays you real money before it robs you.

What the Task Scam Actually Is

The pitch is a work from home job doing micro tasks for a company that supposedly helps apps and online stores boost their engagement. You complete sets of tiny tasks and earn a commission for each one. It is dressed up in the language of the gig economy, which is exactly why it feels plausible.

The reality is a confidence trick with a payout schedule. There is no employer. The tasks do nothing. The platform is a fake dashboard the scammers control completely. The whole machine exists to walk you from a small real payment to a large deposit that disappears.

No legitimate job has ever required you to deposit your own money before you can get paid. That single rule defeats this entire scam.

How the Con Plays Out, Step by Step

  1. The cold message. A recruiter contacts you out of nowhere by text, WhatsApp, or a messaging app. The offer is part time, remote, and pays far too well for how easy it sounds.
  2. The warm up tasks. You complete a few simple tasks, like videos, submit ratings, and your balance goes up. They let you withdraw a small amount, maybe $20 or $50. The money is real. This is the hook.
  3. The trust is set. Once you have pulled real cash out once, your brain marks the platform as legitimate. You tell a friend. You are now all in.
  4. The "combination" tasks appear. Suddenly some high value tasks require you to fund them. They call it prepaying for a task set, topping up your balance, or unlocking a tier. To earn the big commission, you must deposit your own money first.
  5. The negative balance trap. You deposit. The dashboard shows a glitch, a frozen balance, or a task that needs one more deposit to complete. Each time you pay, a new reason appears for why you cannot withdraw yet.
  6. The disappearance. Eventually you stop paying, or you run out of money. The "manager" blocks you. The site goes dark. Everything you deposited is gone, and it was never coming back.

If that structure sounds familiar, it should. It is the same engine behind the AI trading bot scam: a small fake win, a manufactured sense of trust, then escalating deposits you can never withdraw.

Why Even Careful People Fall For It

People assume only the desperate or gullible get caught. Wrong. These operations are industrial. Many are run from compounds staffed by trafficked workers following detailed scripts, with managers and quotas. They are very good at this.

The genius is the real payout. Almost every scam asks you to send money on faith. This one gives you money first. That tiny withdrawal short circuits your skepticism completely, because now you have proof it works. The deposit requests only start after you are already a believer.

It also targets people looking for flexible income: students, parents at home, people between jobs, anyone wanting a side hustle. The offer meets a real need, which makes the bait far easier to swallow.

The Red Flags That Never Lie

  • You were recruited by a random text or chat. Real employers do not cold text strangers offering $300 a day to tap buttons.
  • You have to deposit money to earn money. This is the whole scam in one sentence. Paying to unlock tasks, top up a balance, or complete a "combination" task is never real work.
  • The pay is wildly high for the effort. Liking videos is not worth hundreds a day. If the math makes no sense, it is bait.
  • Everything happens inside a chat app. No real contract, no real company you can verify, no normal hiring process. Just a friendly manager in Telegram or WhatsApp.
  • Crypto is involved. If they want deposits or pay you in crypto, the money becomes nearly impossible to trace or recover. Scammers love it for exactly that reason.
  • Urgency and a group chat. You get added to a group where strangers post screenshots of huge earnings. They are all in on it. The pressure to keep up is manufactured.

What To Do If You Are Already In It

The moment a job asks you to deposit money, the scam is confirmed. Stop immediately. Paying more will not free your balance, because there is no balance.

  • Stop depositing now. Every extra payment is gone the second it lands. Do not chase it.
  • Screenshot everything. The chats, the dashboard, the wallet addresses, the recruiter's profile, every transaction.
  • Report it. File with your national fraud center and, in the United States, the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov. Our guide on how to actually file an FTC fraud report walks through it.
  • Contact your bank or card provider. If you funded it by card or transfer, there may be a narrow window to dispute the charge.
  • Block the recovery scammers. Soon after, someone will message offering to recover your lost money for an upfront fee. That is the same scam targeting you a second time. We cover it in our crypto recovery scam breakdown.

The One Rule That Beats This Scam

You do not need to track the names of every fake platform or recruiter. The story changes constantly. The mechanic never does. A job pays you. You never pay a job.

The instant any opportunity asks you to put your own money in before you can take money out, it is not a job. It is a trap wearing a job's clothes. Learn that one line and every version of this scam falls apart in front of you.
