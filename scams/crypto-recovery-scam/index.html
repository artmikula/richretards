Crypto Recovery Scams: The Con That Hunts Victims Twice

Published June 29, 2026 | By Rich Retards

There is a special cruelty to this one. You already lost money. A fake investment, a romance scam, a rug pull, a fake trading platform. You are angry, embarrassed, and desperate to claw it back. Then a message arrives that sounds like a miracle. "We specialize in recovering stolen crypto. We can get your funds back."

It feels like a lifeline. It is a second hook. The crypto recovery scam exists for one reason: to take a second payment from people who were already robbed. And because the target is already hurting, it works terrifyingly well.

What the Recovery Scam Actually Is

The pitch is a professional sounding service that hunts down and recovers stolen cryptocurrency. They use words like blockchain forensics, asset tracing, and legal recovery team. Some pose as a government agency, a law firm, or even the exchange where you lost the money. Some pretend to be other victims who "got their money back" and want to share the secret.

None of it is real. They cannot recover anything because they were never going to try. The entire interaction is built to extract a fee, then more fees, from someone who has already proven they can be manipulated.

The first scam took your money. The recovery scam takes your hope, and then the rest of your money.

How They Find You

This is the part that unsettles people. How did they know you were scammed? A few ways. Victim lists are bought, sold, and traded among fraud rings, so being scammed once puts you on a target list. If you posted about your loss in a forum, a comment section, or a social media reply, scammers scrape those posts and DM you. And sometimes the recovery scammer is literally the original scammer, circling back under a new name.

If you lost money to something like the AI trading bot scam or a task scam, expect a recovery offer to show up soon after. It is not a coincidence. It is the next stage of the same machine.

How the Con Plays Out

  1. The unsolicited offer. Out of nowhere, a recovery agent contacts you. They already seem to know you were scammed, which makes them feel credible.
  2. The confident promise. They guarantee they can recover your funds. Real investigators never guarantee anything. Certainty is the tell.
  3. The upfront fee. Before any work, you must pay. They call it a retainer, a processing fee, a blockchain tracing cost, or a deposit to unlock your funds.
  4. The fake progress. They show you a dashboard, a tracking report, or a screenshot proving your money has been "located." It is fabricated, just like the original scam's dashboard.
  5. The endless fees. Now there is a tax to release the funds, a legal fee, a bribe for a foreign official, a wallet activation cost. Each payment unlocks only a request for the next one.
  6. The second disappearance. Eventually you stop paying. They vanish. You are now out the original loss plus everything you paid to recover it.

The Red Flags That Never Lie

  • They contacted you first. Legitimate help does not cold message scam victims. If a recovery service found you, it found you to scam you.
  • They guarantee recovery. No honest investigator promises results. Crypto is irreversible, and certainty is a sales tactic.
  • They want an upfront fee. Especially in crypto, gift cards, or wire transfer. Real recovery does not start with you sending more money to a stranger.
  • They claim to be the government or your exchange. Agencies and exchanges do not DM you offering paid recovery. Verify through official channels only.
  • They show a dashboard "proving" your funds are found. The same fake screen trick as the original scam. A picture of your money is not your money.
  • They create urgency. "The funds will be lost forever if you do not act in 24 hours." Pressure exists to stop you thinking.

What Real Recovery Looks Like

Genuine help is slow, official, and never asks you to pay a stranger upfront. Here is the real playbook.

  • Report to the authorities. File with your national fraud center and, in the US, the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov and the FBI internet crime complaint center. Our FTC fraud report guide shows exactly how.
  • Tell your bank or card provider fast. If you funded the original loss by card or transfer, there may be a dispute window. This is your most realistic shot, and it is free.
  • Report to the exchange. If the scam used a real exchange, report the wallet address. They sometimes freeze funds tied to fraud.
  • Document everything and expect no miracle. Realistically, money sent in crypto is usually gone. Anyone telling you otherwise for a fee is lying.

The One Rule That Beats This Scam

If someone contacts you offering to recover money you lost, the answer is no. Every time. Real recovery happens through official channels you initiate yourself, never through a service that slid into your messages with a guarantee and an invoice.

Getting scammed once is not a character flaw. These operations are professional and relentless. But knowing the recovery scam is coming is your armor. When that hopeful message arrives, you will recognize it for exactly what it is: the same wolf, a new costume.
